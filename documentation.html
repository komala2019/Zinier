Business Context

The platform serves a national fiber broadband operator that delivers most installation and repair work through third-party subcontractors.

Operating scale
  • Approximately 6,000 jobs each week: installations, fault repairs and scheduled maintenance.
  • Approximately 70% of work is delivered by 200 independent subcontractor firms.
  • Firms range from three-person outfits to businesses with 400 technicians.
Commercial model
  • The operator pays per completed job against a contract rate card.
  • SLA penalties apply when agreed service levels are missed.
  • Subcontractors operate on thin margins, own their vans and tools, and may also work for competing operators.

Product surfaces and data

  • Operator web console: For regional managers and vendor managers.
  • Subcontractor mobile app: For dispatchers and field technicians, including in poor-signal environments.
  • Operator systems: Work orders, network inventory, service addresses, SLA terms, rate cards and technician certifications.
  • Field-generated data: Job notes, completion photos, failure reasons, access problems and customer no-shows.
  • Third-party and real-time data: Weather, routing, parts, certification, technician location, job status, van stock and same-day cancellations.
The two-sided problem: Subcontractors need sufficient work, fast payment and fewer disputes. The operator needs throughput, quality and cost control. The product must make these goals work together.

2. Presentation

2.1 Design the Field Technician Mobile App and Onboarding Flow

Question: How will a technician complete a job and submit evidence quickly, including when there is no cellular signal?

Answer: Build an offline-first mobile app with a guided, low-friction workflow. The target is under two minutes per job, with minimal typing and large touch targets.

Core experience

  1. Job queue: Show assigned jobs, status and a clear offline indicator.
  2. Job detail: Display the address, work scope, SLA countdown and required evidence.
  3. Guided evidence capture: Capture the optical power meter reading, splice tray and external customer wall box. Attach job ID, timestamp and relevant location context.
  4. Exception reporting: One-tap reporting for blocked access, damaged ducts, customer no-shows or other site issues.
  5. Submit and sync: Save securely on-device when offline and synchronize automatically when connectivity returns.

Onboarding flow

SMS invitation → OTP verification → Plain-language permissions → Download offline job pack → Short optional guided tour → Open first job.

The proposed onboarding target is less than three minutes to first job. The app should use an encrypted local store, queue actions safely and compress photos for unreliable networks.

2.2 Define the Operator Console

Question: How will the operator manage subcontractor performance and respond to requests?

Answer: Provide a web console with two core areas: a performance scorecard across subcontractors and a time-bound request review queue.

Performance scorecard

  • SLA hit rate and job completion status.
  • First-time-fix rate and repeat visit trends.
  • Variation and dispute frequency.
  • Job-to-settlement cycle time.
  • Capacity and performance by firm and region.

Variation and exception review

  • Show dispatched scope next to claimed scope.
  • Display field photos, timestamps, location context and applicable rate-card lines.
  • Provide approve, modify/counter or reject actions; require a reason for rejection.
  • Show a four-hour decision timer and escalation when a request is not reviewed.
Policy decision to resolve: The concept proposes that requests may default in the subcontractor's favor if the operator misses the four-hour window. This needs explicit approval from Operations, Finance and Legal, plus a defined exception and audit process.

2.4 Choose One Workflow Where AI Adds Value

Question: What does the model decide versus a human? What happens when it is wrong, how is success measured, and where should AI not be used?

Answer: Use AI to assist with evidence verification and rate-card matching for additional-work requests. AI prepares a review; it does not make the final financial decision.

Workflow

  1. Capture: Technician submits photos, EXIF metadata, GPS context and work-order ID.
  2. Device pre-check: Detect blur and glare; prompt for a retake when evidence is unusable.
  3. Parallel AI checks: Computer vision/OCR checks trench depth, duct blockage and optical power readings; anti-fraud checks flag possible reused photos or inconsistent location context; contract matching maps the request to the rate card.
  4. Confidence routing: The proposed design uses a confidence threshold of at least 85% and a rate-card delta below £100 to pre-populate a quick-review card. Lower confidence or mismatches go to assisted review.
  5. Human decision: A vendor manager approves, modifies or rejects the request and records the reason.
  6. Record update: The approved rate-card delta is written to the job record with an audit trail.
AI can
  • Extract readings and identify visual evidence.
  • Flag possible duplicate images.
  • Suggest a matching rate-card code.
  • Prioritize cases for human review.
AI must not
  • Deduct money or apply penalties.
  • Automatically suspend or de-rank a subcontractor.
  • Make a binding payment decision.
  • Auto-reject low-confidence or ambiguous cases.

When AI is wrong and how to measure it

Route uncertain results to a human, retain original evidence, explain the reason for each flag, allow the subcontractor to challenge an outcome and audit model performance. Track review time, human override rate, false-positive rate, dispute recurrence and evidence re-submission rate. Proposed targets include reducing triage time by 80%, keeping overrides below 15% and dispute recurrence below 2%; validate these during the pilot.

2.5 Describe Implementation Challenges

Question: What are the technical, adoption and commercial challenges?

Technical: poor connectivity and high photo volume

Challenge: Thousands of photos may need to upload over weak or congested mobile networks.
Response: Compress images on-device, sync metadata first, queue photo uploads in chunks and retry safely. Keep the core workflow available offline.

Adoption: existing WhatsApp-based habits

Challenge: Dispatchers and technicians may resist another app or duplicate administration.
Response: Use SMS deep-link invitations, short training, dispatcher workshops and an immediate benefit: faster evidence-backed decisions and payment.

Commercial: thin subcontractor margins and competing operators

Challenge: A subscription fee or fear of data sharing could lead firms to move technicians to competing operators.
Response: Keep the core app free to subcontractors, enforce strict tenant-level data separation and show each operator only its own work data.

Trust and privacy: location evidence

Challenge: Technicians may interpret location collection as continuous surveillance.
Response: Collect location only when needed for a job event, explain the purpose, limit access and retention, and provide a review path for inaccurate signals.

2.7 Describe the Go-to-Market Strategy

Question: How do we move from the operator's signature to 200 subcontractor firms actively using the product?

Answer: Roll out in four phases, starting with a small group of anchor firms, expanding to the highest-volume partners, then onboarding the long tail before standardizing billing policy.

Phase 1 · Weeks 1–6: Anchor pilot

Three friendly Tier-1 firms, approximately 150 technicians and around 1,000 jobs/week in the Northern region. Validate offline reliability, evidence quality and the payment incentive. Offer guaranteed Net-7 payment for eligible verified jobs.

Phase 2 · Weeks 7–16: Top 25 firms

Onboard the largest 25 firms, representing approximately 60% of volume. Run train-the-dispatcher workshops and launch the dispatcher web portal.

Phase 3 · Weeks 17–26: Remaining 175 firms

Use self-serve SMS deep links, short video guides and low-touch support for smaller firms with 3–15 technicians.

Phase 4 · Week 27 onward: Billing policy

Make digital evidence the standard route for invoice settlement. The proposal suggests a 60-day audit queue for unverified paper invoices and a seven-day payment target for platform-verified invoices. Communicate and approve this policy before enforcement.

Adoption principle: Lead with value—faster payment, less admin and fairer dispute resolution. Do not rely on penalties as the primary adoption mechanism.

2.8 Optional: Other Improvements and Expansions

Question: What could the platform support after the core workflow is proven?

Horizon 2 · Smart operations
  • Predictive van-stock replenishment using job mix, neighborhood demand and distributor availability.
  • Weather-based operational alerts and assisted SLA pause workflows.
  • Capacity forecasting and smarter job allocation across subcontractors.
Horizon 3 · Partner ecosystem
  • Peer-to-peer exchange of spare parts between nearby, non-competing crews.
  • Collective purchasing opportunities for vans, fuel, tools and equipment financing.
  • Partner insights and benchmarking with clear tenant-level data boundaries.

These are later opportunities, not part of the initial release. Prioritize them only after the core evidence, exception and payment workflow demonstrates adoption and measurable value.

3. Roleplay — Pitch to the Operator COO

Assignment task: Pitch the platform to the interviewer, playing the operator's COO.

You manage around 6,000 field jobs every week, with 70% delivered through 200 independent subcontractor firms. Today, job exceptions and supporting evidence are fragmented across messages, calls and delayed invoices. That creates avoidable repeat visits, payment disputes and administrative effort—and puts pressure on relationships with the firms you depend on.

The proposed platform connects a simple, offline-first technician app with an operator console. Technicians can record job progress and evidence in under two minutes. Vendor managers see the original scope, field evidence and rate-card variation together, with a clear four-hour decision target. AI helps organize and check evidence, while people retain control of financial decisions.

The business case estimates £1.45 million in annual gross benefits against £374,400 in annual platform cost, or approximately £1.07 million in net savings. I recommend starting with a six-week pilot involving three subcontractors and approximately 150 technicians. We will measure adoption, repeat visits, dispute resolution time and payment cycle before expanding.

4. Roleplay — Critique the Product

Assignment task: Present a candid critique of the proposed product, including where it could fail and what must be tested.

1. Evidence fraud and false confidence

A technician could submit misleading or reused photos, while GPS may be inaccurate indoors. Location and image signals are not proof on their own. Use multiple signals, human review, audit trails and a challenge process.

2. Legacy integration risk

Enterprise IT may not deliver APIs on the proposed timeline. Validate SFTP/CSV integration with the operator early, agree data ownership and reconciliation, and avoid making field workflows dependent on real-time enterprise availability.

3. Four-hour approval promise may not scale

A high volume of variation requests could overwhelm vendor managers. Pilot the workload, measure arrival patterns, define staffing and escalation, and use AI to prioritize—not silently approve or reject—requests.

4. Adoption may fail if payment benefits are not real

Subcontractors will not change established behavior for a promise that is not honored. Confirm Finance can support the faster payment process and track actual payment performance during the pilot.

5. The financial model may overstate benefits

Savings from fewer repeat visits, lower dispute costs and staff redeployment need evidence. Establish a baseline, define attribution and report realized savings separately from forecast savings.

Closing position: The opportunity is meaningful, but the product should scale only after the pilot validates technician adoption, integration feasibility, the four-hour operating commitment and the financial assumptions.
