1. Overview

The broadband operator manages approximately 6,000 installation, repair and maintenance jobs each week. Around 70% are completed by a network of 200 subcontractor firms, ranging from small teams to companies with hundreds of technicians.

Job updates, site exceptions and supporting evidence are often shared through fragmented channels. Variation claims can arrive weeks after the work, creating disputes, manual reconciliation and delayed payments.

Product goal

Create one reliable workflow for job updates, evidence capture, exception handling and payment decisions—improving operational visibility for the operator and making work and payment more predictable for subcontractors.

Scope

In scope
  • Technician mobile app with offline support.
  • Operator web console and subcontractor performance view.
  • Job evidence and variation requests.
  • Approval workflow and audit history.
  • Integration with operator systems and selected third parties.
  • AI-assisted evidence checks.
Later opportunities
  • Predictive van-stock replenishment.
  • Advanced capacity and job allocation.
  • Parts exchange between nearby crews.
  • Partner purchasing and financing services.

3. Product Experience

3.1 Technician mobile app

  1. Sign in: Join through an SMS invitation and OTP verification.
  2. View jobs: See assigned jobs, address, scope, status and SLA information.
  3. Capture evidence: Follow prompts to photograph the optical power meter reading, splice tray and external wall box.
  4. Report an exception: Record blocked access, damaged ducts, customer no-shows or additional work.
  5. Submit: Save securely offline and sync automatically when connectivity returns.

3.2 Operator web console

  • Monitor job volume, SLA performance, first-time-fix rate and repeat visits by firm and region.
  • Review variation requests with original scope, submitted evidence and contract rate-card details side by side.
  • Approve, modify or reject requests with a recorded reason and decision history.
  • Track pending decisions against a proposed four-hour turnaround target.

Reliability principles: The field workflow must not depend on continuous connectivity. Use local storage, background sync, retry queues, idempotency, monitoring and reconciliation. Non-critical third-party outages should not block job completion.

6. AI-Assisted Evidence Review

AI reduces repetitive checks and helps prioritize requests. It does not make final payment, penalty or subcontractor-performance decisions.

1. Capture and quality check

Collect job photos and metadata; detect blur or glare before upload and request a retake when needed.

2. Run evidence checks

Use computer vision and OCR for meter readings and site evidence; flag possible duplicate images or inconsistent location context.

3. Match the contract

Compare the requested work and price with the relevant rate-card code.

4. Route for review

The proposed design uses confidence of at least 85% and a rate-card delta below £100 to pre-populate a quick-review card. Lower-confidence or mismatched cases go to assisted review.

5. Human decision

A vendor manager approves, modifies or rejects the request. The decision and approved value are saved to the job record.

Provide explainable flags, human override, an appeal path and an audit trail. Never use AI alone to deduct payment, apply penalties or suspend a subcontractor. The proposed 85% and £100 thresholds require pilot validation.

9. Rollout Plan

Phase 1 · Weeks 1–6: Anchor pilot

Three Tier-1 firms, approximately 150 technicians and around 1,000 jobs per week in the Northern region. Validate app usability, offline sync, evidence quality and Net-7 payment for eligible verified jobs.

Phase 2 · Weeks 7–16: Largest partners

Onboard the top 25 firms, representing approximately 60% of job volume. Run dispatcher training and launch the web portal.

Phase 3 · Weeks 17–26: Network expansion

Onboard the remaining 175 firms using SMS links, short videos and lightweight support.

Phase 4 · Week 27 onward: Standardize billing

Make digital evidence the standard billing route. The proposal suggests a 60-day audit queue for unverified paper invoices and a seven-day payment target for verified invoices, subject to policy approval and clear exceptions.
