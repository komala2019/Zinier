Improving Broadband Contractor Operations

A digital platform to improve coordination between a broadband operator and its subcontractor network, strengthen job evidence, accelerate payment decisions and reduce operational waste.

1. Business Context and Problem

The broadband operator manages approximately 6,000 installation and repair visits each week. Around 70% of the field work is delivered through a network of 200 subcontractor companies.

Today, operational exceptions and supporting evidence are often shared through informal channels such as WhatsApp, while payment disputes may not surface until weeks after the work is completed. This creates avoidable delays, manual effort and friction between the operator and its partners.

Current process: where it breaks down

  1. Unexpected work: A technician encounters an issue at a property, such as a damaged underground duct, requiring additional time and materials.
  2. Evidence is fragmented: The technician sends photos and an update to their supervisor through an informal channel. The operator may not receive the information promptly.
  3. Job status becomes inaccurate: The operator's system may continue to treat the job as delayed, even when the delay is caused by a site issue outside the technician's control.
  4. Disputes arrive late: Additional charges may be submitted weeks later, without consistent evidence or prior approval.
  5. Partner relationships suffer: Delayed decisions and disputed payments reduce subcontractor confidence and may lead experienced teams to prioritize other clients.
Core problem to solve

Create a shared, timely and auditable process for recording job progress, raising exceptions, validating evidence and making payment decisions.

2. Stakeholders and Incentives

The platform must create value for both sides. Adoption depends on making the process efficient for field teams while giving the operator the controls and visibility it needs.

Broadband operator

  • Priorities: Reliable installations, fewer customer complaints, cost control and clear operational visibility.
  • Current pain points: Late claims, inconsistent evidence, manual reconciliation and limited insight into subcontractor performance.
  • Adoption requirement: Integration with existing systems and minimal duplicate data entry.

Subcontractors and technicians

  • Priorities: A simple field workflow, fair treatment, predictable work and timely payment.
  • Current pain points: Connectivity issues, repeated administration, disputed claims and penalties for delays outside their control.
  • Adoption requirement: A free, reliable app that takes less than two minutes per job and works offline.
Proposed value exchange
  • The broadband operator funds the platform in return for improved control, reduced waste and better evidence.
  • The app is free for subcontractor companies and technicians.
  • Complete and verified job submissions are eligible for a seven-day payment target, compared with the current cycle of up to 45 days.

3. Product Solution

The solution has two connected experiences: a mobile app for technicians in the field and a web console for the operator's operations team.

3.1 Field Technician Mobile App

  • Fast job updates: A guided workflow with four primary actions, large touch targets and minimal typing. Target completion time is under two minutes per job.
  • Offline capability: Job updates and evidence are stored securely on the device when connectivity is unavailable, then synchronized when a connection returns.
  • Guided photo capture: The app requests three evidence photos: the optical light meter reading (for example, -18.2 dBm), the wire splice tray and the external customer wall box.
  • Exception reporting: A technician can flag a blocked job, such as a locked gate or damaged duct. The event is time-stamped and routed to the operator so the job status can be reviewed.

3.2 Operator Web Console

  • Subcontractor performance view: A consolidated view of performance across all 200 partner companies, supporting evidence-based work allocation and operational reviews.
  • Time-bound decisions: Additional-work requests enter a managed approval queue with a proposed four-hour decision target and clear escalation when no decision is made.
  • Evidence review: Review the original job scope, the additional work requested and the supporting photos together, with a clear approval or rejection action.
Important policy decision: The original proposal includes an automatic approval outcome when a request is not reviewed within four hours. This should be treated as a business rule requiring explicit agreement from Finance, Operations and Legal before implementation.

4. AI Capabilities and Governance

AI is used to assist evidence review and reduce repetitive manual checks. It should provide recommendations and signals, not make punitive decisions about workers or subcontractors.

AI-assisted capabilities

  • Extract meter readings from submitted photos.
  • Assess whether submitted trench evidence appears to meet the required depth, such as 30 cm, subject to image quality and human verification.
  • Flag possible duplicate or reused photos.
  • Compare additional-work requests with the relevant pricing rules.

Controls and human oversight

  • AI does not deduct money from a worker or subcontractor.
  • AI does not suspend, ban or penalize a company.
  • Payment decisions remain subject to an authorized human approval process.
  • AI flags should be explainable, reviewable and auditable, with a way to challenge incorrect results.

6. Implementation Roadmap

Use a phased rollout to validate the workflow, prove operational value and reduce disruption before onboarding the full subcontractor network.

1
Weeks 1–6: Pilot

Start with three willing subcontractors and approximately 150 technicians in one city. Validate the mobile workflow, offline behavior, evidence quality and seven-day payment process. Resolve critical usability and integration issues.

2
Weeks 7–16: High-volume partners

Onboard the 25 largest subcontractors, representing approximately 60% of job volume. Provide focused training for dispatchers and operational leads.

3
Weeks 17–26: Network expansion

Onboard the remaining 175 subcontractors using short training videos, direct onboarding links and support materials. Track adoption and support needs by partner.

4
Week 27 onward: Standard operating model

Make digital evidence submission the standard route for faster payment processing. Define a fair, communicated process for exceptions and manual submissions rather than relying on an unreviewed penalty queue.

7. Key Risks and Mitigations

Risk 1: Evidence could be captured away from the job site.

Mitigation: Validate capture-time location, timestamps and job context; detect suspicious patterns and provide a review path. Avoid relying on a single GPS signal as definitive proof.

Risk 2: Integration with legacy enterprise systems takes longer than planned.

Mitigation: Start with scheduled CSV exchange over SFTP where appropriate, while defining secure access, reconciliation, monitoring and a phased path to API integration.

Risk 3: Technicians perceive the app as employee surveillance.

Mitigation: Do not use continuous background location tracking. Collect location only when needed for a job event, explain the purpose clearly and define retention and access policies.

Risk 4: The operations team receives too many approval requests.

Mitigation: Use configurable thresholds and risk-based prioritization. Low-value, high-confidence requests may be eligible for controlled auto-approval only after policy approval, with sampling, audit trails and an appeal process.

8. Executive Summary

The current subcontractor operating model relies on fragmented communication and delayed evidence, creating avoidable disputes, repeat visits and administrative effort. The proposed platform connects a field technician app with an operator web console so job updates, exceptions and supporting evidence are captured at the point of work and reviewed through a consistent process.

The mobile experience is designed to work offline and take less than two minutes per job. The operator console provides performance visibility, structured exception handling and faster evidence review. AI can assist with image and pricing checks, while payment and performance decisions remain governed by clear human oversight.

The financial model forecasts £1.07 million in annual net savings against an estimated annual platform cost of £374,400. A six-week pilot with three subcontractors is the proposed first step to validate adoption, operational impact and the assumptions behind the business case.
